DeskLink connects your Android phone to your Linux computer. Pair once by scanning a QR code, and the two work together at home or away from it. No account, no ads, no tracking.

<b>Shared terminals</b>
- Open terminals that run on your computer and pick them up on your phone, or start one on the phone and keep typing at your desk.
- One tap starts Claude Code or Codex in a terminal on your computer, so you can keep an eye on an agent from anywhere.
- Dictate into the terminal with your phone's speech recognizer.

<b>Remote control and screens</b>
- See your computer's screen on the phone and control it with touch, keyboard and mouse.
- Show your phone's screen on the computer.
- Use the phone as a touchpad and keyboard.

<b>Everyday integration</b>
- Notifications from your phone appear on the computer, and you can reply from there.
- Shared clipboard: copy on one device, paste on the other.
- Send files and links both ways, and keep a folder (the Bucket) in step between the phone and the computer.
- Notes, snippets, contacts and calls on the computer, and your phone's microphone or sound on the computer when you allow it.
- The computer's pointer and keyboard can carry on onto the phone's screen, and the phone can take a photo, a scan or a QR text into whatever you are doing on the computer.
- Battery level, find my phone, lock and unlock the computer from your phone.

<b>Works anywhere, privately</b>
- On the same Wi-Fi, the devices talk directly over an encrypted connection.
- Away from home, they find each other through the DeskLink relay. The connection setup is end-to-end encrypted: the relay sees IP addresses and device fingerprints, never your content.
- No account and no sign-up. Pairing keys stay on your devices.

DeskLink needs the DeskLink desktop app on your Linux computer.

<b>Permissions</b>
- Accessibility service (optional, explained and agreed to first): lets the computer control your phone, type into apps and carry its pointer onto the screen. DeskLink does not collect or share data through it.
- Notification access (optional): to show your phone's notifications on the computer.
- Contacts, phone and call permissions (optional, off by default): to share contacts and use calls from the computer; a call the computer asks for is placed only when you tap Allow.
- Microphone (optional): to stream your phone's microphone or sound to the computer when you allow it.
- Screen capture: asked each time you share the phone's screen.
- Camera: to scan the pairing QR code.
